Two announced routes to the Riemann Hypothesis: a source-grounded comparison of proof obligations

Pablo Nogueira Grossi · G6 LLC, Newark, NJ · ORCID 0009-0000-6496-2186 · g6llc@proton.me

No verdict. This paper does not say whether either announced proof is correct. It records what each text proves, what it announces, and which implications still need verification. A replay with the same Lean kernel is not an independent kernel check. Not affiliated with the replay's maintainer, with the claims' authors, or with Anthropic.

Abstract

Two announcements of 20 November 2026 claim the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) by unrelated means. Alpöge's route, as reconstructed by Allahyar, reduces RH to a one-sided window estimate on a weighted prime sum and obtains that estimate from chains of theta lifts, with a Lean 4 release of 175,187 files. Masullo's route, in a 940-page manuscript, computes a trace formula on the idele class group and deduces that the logarithmic derivative of every Hecke L-function is holomorphic on Re s > 1/2. The arguments differ in engine and agree in ending: each produces nonvanishing in the open right half of the critical strip and finishes with the functional equation.

The paper separates three levels of evidence. Part I states each announced proof from its text and records which inputs it takes as given and which parts are machine-checked. Part II treats what follows from RH or is equivalent to it: twenty published statements, seven of them equivalences, and finite computations on the equivalent criteria. Part III reports exact identities that need no assumption about RH: the spectral zeta function of the round three-sphere, its Hopf twist and lens-space quotient, the Eisenstein series of the Picard group, and a local nodal-crossing theorem at z = i on the modular surface. Part IV compares the proof obligations of the two announcements in a ledger and isolates the finite-codimension test-function restriction in Masullo's Theorem 13.3.1.1.

Part I. Two announced proofs

The two claims and their sources

Alpöge's announcement claims RH, and the same method is said to give the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions. The released tree has 175,187 Lean files and about 17.4 million lines and pins Lean 4.33.1 and Mathlib revision 0df444a360ea. Allahyar's exposition proves the analytic half in full and describes the arithmetic half in terms of results read from the Lean files. Its section 11 states that the full dependency tree and the axioms of the final RH declaration have not been independently checked for the exposition, and that only the reflection module, which gives RH from right-half-plane nonvanishing, was compiled separately and found to use only propositional extensionality, classical choice and quotient soundness.

Masullo's manuscript works over an arbitrary number field F and claims the location of the zeros of all Hecke L-functions L(s, χ) with a bound on multiplicity. Its main tool is the Trace Formula, Theorem 12.5.1.1, stated for test functions φ in D(CF) with φ(CF1) = 0. The deduction of the zero-location theorems is given as an overview in section 1.4.5 and is meant to be carried out in Chapter 13. In the supplied manuscript, Chapter 13 (page 522) consists of section headings, Theorem 13.3.1.1 (a restated Trace Formula counting zeros with multiplicity capped at [F : Q], whose test functions must also satisfy [F : Q] vanishing conditions on derivatives of an integral over R>0, followed by a combination with an unresolved reference “Theorem ??”), and Theorem 13.3.1.2 (Divisor of L(s, χ)), whose statement is marked “Insert statement” in a footnote, with a further footnote reading “State and prove the Main Theorems”.

The common ending

Both routes show that L(s, χ) has no zeros in Re s > 1/2 and use the functional equation, which exchanges s and 1 − s, to exclude zeros in Re s < 1/2. For ζ this leaves the nontrivial zeros on Re s = 1/2. Masullo states it in section 1.4.5; Allahyar's section 4 carries it out for ζ; Alpöge's released reflection module formalizes it. A single Lean lemma, no zeros of ζ in Re s > 1/2 implies RH, therefore serves both routes, and everything that distinguishes them lies upstream of it.

Route A: Alpöge, as reconstructed by Allahyar

Analytic half. Put S(x) = Σn≤x Λ(n)(x/n − 1) and G(x) = S(x) − x log x + (1 + γ)x. Arithmetic input 1.1 asserts that there are A ≥ 0 and ε0 > 0 such that for every 0 < ε < ε0 and every Y0 ≥ 2 some Y ≥ Y0 satisfies G(x) ≤ x1/2+Aε for all Y ≤ x ≤ Y3/2. Theorem 1.2 states that this implies every zero of ζ other than a negative even integer has real part 1/2. The Mellin transform of G with the weight (x/n − 1)+ is

∫1∞ G(x) x−s−1 dx = −(ζ′/ζ)(s) / (s(s−1)) − 1/(s−1)2 + (1+γ)/(s−1),

in which the pole at s = 1 cancels and each nontrivial zero of ζ remains a pole. (This identity was re-derived directly: ∫n∞(x/n−1)x−s−1dx = n−s/(s(s−1)), ∫1∞x log x·x−s−1dx = 1/(s−1)2.)

Arithmetic half. The window estimate is the output of a construction in sections 5 to 10. A chain of theta lifts through O(2, n) and a unitary tower accumulates divisibility of periods at finite primes; averaging over translates turns the local estimates into weights (a/pi − w)+ log p; a seesaw identifies the periods of many characters with maps into one Hermitian lattice, and a relative trace formula shows these images nearly span it. Allahyar describes the method as Stepanov-style. The chain has square-root length because two costs, x/m and m, are balanced at m = x1/2 (section 6, Proposition 6.1). The estimates are controlled by Selmer-group lengths, a cyclotomic tower at exceptional primes, and rank and height comparisons on an auxiliary cover.

What is taken as given. Section 11 lists the arithmetic inputs the exposition draws from the Lean files: existence and intersection theory of the deformation model, automorphy with local conditions, control of the dual tower module, global matching of integral theta periods, quantitative lattice counts at fixed corank, and height and arithmetic-degree estimates on the auxiliary models. Sections 2 to 4 and the Fourier identity and exponent derivation of sections 5 and 6 are proved in the exposition itself.

Route B: Masullo

The setting is the idele class group CF = AF×/F×. Chapters 2 and 3 build adelic θ-functions and idelic zeta functions; Chapters 4 to 7 construct spaces of them with topologies defined by periodic Schauder bases, and Chapter 7 treats polar multiplicities, poles of S-idelic zeta functions, Paley–Wiener levels, nuclearity and strong co-primality. Chapters 8 to 12 prove the Trace Formula, Theorem 12.5.1.1: for φ in D(CF) with φ(CF1) = 0, a sum of archimedean and non-archimedean local orbital integrals equals M(φ)(|·|1/2) + M(φ)(|·|−1/2) minus a sum over characters c of N(c) M(φ)(c|·|−1/2).

The deduction in section 1.4.5 applies the formula to the family φs(t) = ts for t > 1 and 0 for t < 1 (section 1.4.6). The result is that the logarithmic derivative of L(s + 1/2, χ), after subtracting the pole terms δ(1/(s + 1/2) + 1/(s − 1/2)), is holomorphic on Re s > a for every a > 0, hence on Re s > 0 apart from s = 1/2, hence L has no zeros on Re s > 1/2. The manuscript states that Weil's positivity criterion is not used, and that the vanishing condition φ(CF1) = 0 cannot be removed before GRH is proved.

Inputs. Section 1.7.1 states that Theorem 1.7.1.1, on polar multiplicities, is used to select normed topologies so that certain coefficient functionals are continuous, and that the Q-linear independence of the logarithms of the primes pervades these choices (PDF page 34). The same section states that Theorem 1.7.1.1 has no useful analog for a function field of positive characteristic (page 35).

Part II. Consequences, equivalences and finite checks

A statement RH ⇒ P becomes unconditional if an RH proof is accepted and gives no support to that proof. A statement RH ⇔ P is stronger in one respect: a counterexample to P would refute RH, so a finite search for one is a falsification test.

Lemma: the largest order in signed-permutation groups

Lemma. Let gB(n) and gD(n) be the largest orders of elements of the hyperoctahedral group Bn and of its index-two subgroup Dn. Then gB(n) = 2g(n) for all n ≥ 1, and gD(n) = 2g(n) for all n ≥ 5.

Proof. A signed k-cycle has order k when its sign is positive and 2k when negative, so the order of a signed permutation divides lcm(siki) with si in {1, 2}, which divides 2·lcm(ki). Hence gB(n) ≤ 2g(n). For the reverse inequality take σ in Sn of order g(n) and negate one cycle whose length has the largest 2-adic valuation v. The 2-part of the order goes from 2v to 2v+1 and the odd part is unchanged, so gB(n) ≥ 2g(n). For Dn the number of negated cycles must be even, so negate a second cycle as well. Negating a cycle of length k raises its 2-part to 2v2(k)+1, at most 2v+1, so the order is still 2g(n). A second cycle exists once the optimal permutation has at least two cycles, and for n ≥ 5 a single n-cycle has order n < g(n). □

A direct enumeration over all cycle types confirms gB = 2g for every n ≤ 30 and gD = 2g for every 5 ≤ n ≤ 30; for n = 1 to 4 it gives gD = 1, 2, 4, 6 against 2g = 2, 4, 6, 8 (bd.py). The proof is the general argument; the enumeration is a consistency check, and no Lean formalization is written. The lift changes only the prime 2, so the lifted Landau criterion is the original with a doubled left side and gives no new inequality.

Part III. Spectral and nodal geometry

This part records exact identities and finite computations around a lift to the round three-sphere and to the modular surface. None gives a new test of RH.

Spectrum on S3

Identify S3 with SU(2). Under left and right translation L2(S3) = ⊕k≥1 Vk⊗Vk, where Vk is the irreducible representation of dimension k, and −Δ + 1 acts on the k-th summand as k2. The eigenvalue k2 has multiplicity k2, so the spectral zeta function is

Σk≥1 k2 (k2)−s = ζ(2s − 2).

A zero ρ of ζ appears at s = (ρ+2)/2, and RH is equivalent to all nontrivial zeros of this function lying on Re s = 5/4. Both statements are proved in Lean 4 (checks/S3Zeta.lean, theorems spectral_zeta_eq and RH_iff_spectral), with axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound only. This is a change of variables: integer frequencies with polynomial multiplicities always give ζ at a shifted argument.

Twisting by a group element g acting on one factor weights the k-th summand by the trace χk(g) of Vk, while the other factor contributes the multiplicity k. For g = diag(i, −i), the Hopf rotation of order 4, the weights of Vk are eimπ/2 with m = −(k−1), −(k−3), …, k−1 and their sum is sin(kπ/2) = χ−4(k). The twisted spectral zeta function is therefore Σ k χ−4(k) k−2s = L(2s − 1, χ−4), with zeros at Re s = 3/4. For the lens space S3/Z4, whose eigenspaces are the Z4-invariants, the spectral zeta function is (1/4) Σj=0..3 Σk k χk(gj) k−2s. The elements 1 and g2 = −1 (acting on Vk as (−1)k−1) give ζ(2s−2) and (1 − 23−2s)ζ(2s−2), and g and g3 give L(2s−1, χ−4) each, so the total is

½ [ (1 − 22−2s) ζ(2s − 2) + L(2s − 1, χ−4) ],

a sum whose zeros carry no information. These are derived identities. A numerical evaluation at s = 3 is a consistency check: the twisted sum and L(5, χ−4) both equal 0.996157828077, and the lens-space sum and the closed form both equal 1.00541793 (lifts_check.py). The plain and twisted spectra give the two factors of the Dedekind zeta function of Q(i), each at a shifted argument, and S3 has no spectral line on which zeros are pinned.

The Picard group on H3

At the point P = (0, 1) the Eisenstein series for PSL2(Z[i]) is a sum over coprime Gaussian pairs of (N(c) + N(d))−s. Summing all pairs gives Σ r4(n) n−s = 8(1 − 41−s) ζ(s) ζ(s−1), and dividing by the Dedekind zeta function ζ(s)L(s, χ−4) of Q(i) gives

8 (1 − 41−s) ζ(s − 1) / L(s, χ−4).

A lattice count at s = 4 gives 9.5720198 against 9.5720211, the difference being the truncated tail. On H3 the spectral line is Re s = 1, while the zeros of ζ(s−1) lie at s = 1 + ρ with real part 3/2. The only zeros on the spectral line come from the factor 1 − 41−s, at t = πk/log 2 (4.532, 9.065, …), and say nothing about ζ.

The modular surface at z = i

The completed Eisenstein series E*(z, s) = π−sΓ(s)ζ(2s)E(z, s) satisfies E(i, s) = 2ζ(s)L(s, χ−4)/ζ(2s), hence

E*(i, s) = 2 π−s Γ(s) ζ(s) L(s, χ−4) = 2 ΛQ(i)(s),

which is real on the critical line (checked to 10−33 at t = 3, 10 and the first zero of ζ; nodal_check.py).

Proposition (local nodal crossing under nondegeneracy). Let ft(z) = E*(z, 1/2 + it) and F(t) = ft(i). (a) For every real t the gradient of ft at i vanishes and ∂xyft(i) = 0. (b) If F(t0) = 0, the Hessian of ft0 at i is a(t0)·diag(1, −1) with a(t0) = ∂xxft0(i). (c) If moreover a(t0) ≠ 0, the nodal set of ft0 near i consists of two smooth branches tangent to y − 1 = ±x, meeting orthogonally; if also F′(t0) ≠ 0, the branches reconnect as t passes t0.

Proof. (a) z ↦ −1/z fixes i with derivative −Id there, which forces the gradient to vanish; the symmetry E(−z̄) = E(z) kills the mixed derivative. (b) The eigenvalue equation Δft = −(1/4 + t2)ft gives fxx + fyy = 0 at a zero. (c) The Hessian has one positive and one negative eigenvalue, so the Morse lemma gives the saddle form, and F′(t0) ≠ 0 gives a transverse one-parameter unfolding. □ No hypothesis on RH is used.

Numerics (nodal_check.py): at the first zero of ζ, f ≈ 5.5×10−40, the gradient and mixed term are 0, a ≈ 2.415×10−7, the trace over a is ≈ 10−31, and F′ ≈ −1.14×10−9. Nondegeneracy a ≠ 0 and F′ ≠ 0 holds at the first four zeros of ζ and the first two zeros of L(s, χ−4). The values are small because of the exponential decay of Γ on the critical line; they are numerical, not certified, and rest on a truncated Fourier expansion. On (0.5, 40] the function t ↦ E*(i, 1/2 + it) changes sign 20 times, equal to the 6 zeros of ζ plus the 14 zeros of L(s, χ−4) in that range, with no common zero. What remains open is a(t0) ≠ 0 at every critical-line zero.

Counting these crossings is the same as counting sign changes of the completed Dedekind zeta function of Q(i) on the critical line. It adds no information beyond RH for ζ and L(s, χ−4). The crossing mechanism needs the spectral line and the critical line to coincide, which happens on H2 (spectral line Re s = 1/2) and not on H3 (Re s = 1) or S3 (no spectral line).

Part IV. Synthesis and proof obligations

Common endpoint and what separates the routes

Both announcements end with right-half-plane nonvanishing followed by the functional equation, and both use test functions cut off at 1. Nothing in the supplied texts relates the arithmetic window estimate of Route A to the trace-formula test functions of Route B, and the existence of a trace formula in each construction does not make them do the same work. Whether the window estimate can be recovered as a family of test-function inequalities inside Masullo's framework is an open question.

The finite-codimension restriction in Theorem 13.3.1.1

Theorem 12.5.1.1 is stated for φ in D(CF) with φ(CF1) = 0. Theorem 13.3.1.1, the form used to count zeros, is stated for φ satisfying, for every character χ and every integer 0 ≤ k ≤ [F : Q] − 1, the condition (13.3.1.2): the k-th derivative in s, at s = −i x0(χ), of the integral over R>0 of χ(τ(t)) ts times the integral over CF1 of φ(τ(t)g)χ(g) dμ1(g), against dt/t, vanishes. These conditions cut out a subspace A of codimension [F : Q] in the manuscript's wording; the manuscript states that both sides of (13.3.1.3) converge on A and are continuous for the subspace topology there. The identity counts each zero c with weight Nspec(c) = min(N(c), [F : Q]). For F = Q this weight is 1 at every zero, so the identity detects zeros without weighting by multiplicity.

The deduction of section 1.4.5 uses φs(t) = ts on t > 1, and section 1.4.6 describes the passage as proving the formula for test functions supported on [1 + ε, ∞), extending it to those supported on (0, 1 − ε], and then to all φ with φ(CF1) = 0 by continuity. A complete proof must show three things. First, that approximants to φs can be taken inside A, or that φs splits as an element of A plus a finite-dimensional remainder whose contribution to every term of (13.3.1.3) is computed explicitly. Second, that the approximants converge to φs in the topology on A in which both sides of (13.3.1.3) are continuous, uniformly enough in s on compact subsets of Re s > a. Third, that the pole terms obtained in the limit are exactly the logarithmic-derivative terms of section 1.4.5, so that holomorphy of L′/L on Re s > 0 away from s = 1/2 follows, with the multiplicity truncation accounted for. None of the three is carried out in the supplied text, because Chapter 13 is incomplete there.

Scope and priority

A literature search by web query did not identify an earlier technical comparison that places the arithmetic verification obligations of the Alpöge–Allahyar route alongside the test-function and trace-formula obligations of Masullo's approach. This is a statement about what the search found, not a claim that none exists; no exhaustive priority search was made, and the pages returned were not examined or cited.

Conclusion

Three outcomes stand apart. First, the two announced proofs share a precise analytic endpoint, nonvanishing in the open right half of the critical strip followed by the functional equation, and differ in everything upstream: the checked boundary of Route A is its analytic half and its reflection module, and that of Route B is its Trace Formula as written, with Chapter 13 and the approximation in the finite-codimension space still to be supplied. Second, the twenty published consequences, seven of them equivalences with RH, make a large body of results depend on either announcement without supporting it; the equivalences are the cheapest falsification tests, and every finite check run on them agrees with RH. Third, the local nodal-crossing statement at z = i holds without any assumption about RH, and the lifts to S3, its Hopf twist, the lens space and H3 produce the same pair of factors, ζ and L(s, χ−4), at shifted arguments, with the spectral line and the critical line coinciding only on H2. None of these results proves or refutes RH.
